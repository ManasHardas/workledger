## Cost model

**Input corpus, measured on the operator's own machine** (E-C5): **34.3M tokens of session transcript per developer per 30-day month** — one human, one agent tool, 17 active days out of 35. A floor, not a ceiling. Extraction output modeled at 5% of input (the §12 entities). Storage and embeddings round to zero: embedding the whole corpus costs **$0.69/dev/month** (E-C2). The LLM pass is the entire cost.

| Extraction model | Input $ | Output $ | **COGS/dev/mo** | Batched (−50%) | At 10× fleet volume |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Haiku 4.5 ($1/$5) | 34.32 | 8.58 | **$42.90** | $21.45 | $429.00 |
| Sonnet 5 ($2/$10) | 68.64 | 17.16 | **$85.80** | $42.90 | $858.00 |
| Opus 5 ($5/$25) | 171.60 | 42.90 | **$214.50** | $107.25 | $2,145.00 |
| gpt-5.6-terra ($2/$12) | 68.64 | 20.59 | **$89.23** | $44.62 | $892.32 |
| gpt-5.6-luna ($0.20/$1.20) | 6.86 | 2.06 | **$8.92** | $4.46 | $89.23 |

**Price required to clear a margin bar, at Haiku-tier COGS ($42.90):**
50% GM → **$86/seat/mo** · 60% GM → **$107** · 75% GM → **$172** · 80% GM → **$215**.
At Sonnet-tier COGS ($85.80): 50% GM → **$172** · 75% GM → **$343**.

**Set that against what the market pays** (E-C13–E-C18): Linear Business **$16** · Unblocked Platform **$29** · Copilot Pro+ **$39** · Cursor Teams **$40** · Claude Team Standard **$20–25**. The entire issue tracker costs $16/seat. This product must charge **5× Linear** to clear a 50% margin on the *cheapest* model that could plausibly do the extraction — for a layer that ships no code, closes no ticket, and renders no UI anyone opens daily.

**The vise.** The only comfortable row is `gpt-5.6-luna` at $8.92 — nano-tier. That is exactly the configuration producing the proposal's own Risk #3 (*"a DB of mediocre LLM summaries"*) and Risk #4 (*"retrieval not trustworthy"*). §8 demands separating "agent thinks X" from "the organization has decided X," with evidence, alternatives and rejection reasons attached — judgment, not summarization. **The model cheap enough to run continuously isn't good enough to produce authoritative provenance; the model good enough costs more than the seat.**

**Two omitted line items.** *(1) Re-extraction.* §12 calls the entity set "hypotheses" and §19 asks what the primitive even is. Every answer change reprocesses the corpus: one re-pass over a 50-dev, 12-month archive is 20.6B input tokens = **$41,184 at Sonnet, $20,592 at Haiku**. You cannot iterate on the data model once you have customers — a cash-denominated coupling between "still learning the primitive" and "insolvent." *(2) Retrieval lands on the customer's bill.* `get_relevant_context()` per session start (§13), at ~159 sessions/dev/month, adds ~1.6M input tokens/dev/month to a Claude Code bill already at $150–250 (E-C3). The buyer sees the memory layer on *two* invoices.

**Pre-empting "inference gets 10× cheaper every year."** True per token. But E-C4 is the counter-fact from this exact market: Anthropic's *observed* cost per developer per active day went **$6 → $13, up 2.2×, in ~14 months** — driven by a model upgrade, the very mechanism meant to make things cheaper. Volume grew faster than price fell. E-C6 multiplies it ~7× again for agent teams. Net cost per developer has moved the wrong way for the entire observable history of this market.

**The structural kill.** COGS scales with agent activity; revenue scales with human seats. The thesis is *10–12 humans + 100–200 agents*. If that world arrives, transcript volume per **human** rises an order of magnitude while billable humans stay flat: Haiku-tier extraction **$429/human/month**, Sonnet-tier **$858** (E-C6 alone justifies 7×). **This product gets more expensive per unit of revenue exactly as its founding thesis comes true.** That is inverse operating leverage, and no pricing page fixes it.

| **Atlassian** | Enterprise procurement and incumbency above 500 people | **Teamwork Graph** (150B objects); **`twg` CLI for Claude Code / Codex / Gemini / Cursor**, updated 2026-09-04; TWG tools in Rovo MCP; Rovo Dev **$20/dev/mo** with pooled credits billed for graph **retrieval** as well as inference (E-C36) | Already selling graph-context-to-agents at $20/dev. Needs only to announce to make the buyer defer two quarters. |
| **OpenAI** | Codex telemetry, AGENTS.md gravity, ChatGPT Enterprise identity | Codex memories (per-user, `~/.codex/memories/`, off by default); AGENTS.md global+project scopes, **no org scope**; org-shared container caches — the thinnest position of the seven | Mirrors Anthropic for parity. Neither can afford the other to own org memory, which guarantees both build it and **neither charges for it.** |

**Two things this table settles.** First, the pricing question is already answered by the people who own the surfaces: **Linear states agents "do not count as billable users" and passes tokens through with "no markup"** (E-C35); Amp includes agent usage 1:1 with the subscription (E-C18); nobody sells a per-agent seat (E-C37). The category has independently converged on *not* monetizing agent state. Second, every asset that makes this product work — the session stream, the org identity boundary, the artifact graph, the channel onto developer machines — is owned by someone else. The operator is a *processor* of other people's data between a model vendor upstream and a tracker downstream, with no proprietary input and no distribution: the weakest seat in the value chain.

## Failure modes

### FM-C1 — Inverse operating leverage: COGS scales with agents, revenue scales with humans

**Mechanism.** Extraction over every agent session costs $42.90/dev/mo at the cheapest credible model and $85.80 at Sonnet, against a measured corpus of 34.3M tokens/dev/month (cost model above; E-C5). Live comparable seats are $16–$40 (E-C13–E-C17). Clearing even a16z's 50–60% AI gross-margin band (E-C20) requires $86–$107/seat — above Cursor Teams, above Unblocked, at Claude Team *Premium* level — for an accessory layer. Then the thesis makes it worse: 100–200 agents per 10–12 humans takes COGS to $429–$858 per *billable human* per month.

**The market has already priced this.** Unblocked — the closest funded analogue, selling "decision-grade context" over code, conversations, issues and docs — has $30M and ~34 people and lists at **$19–$29/user/month** (E-C17, E-C26). A team with 34× the operator's headcount has concluded the ceiling here is $29/seat, which is **1/3 of my Sonnet-tier COGS**. Either they extract far less than "every agent session" — in which case continuous extraction is the expensive part and it is optional — or they run negative gross margin on inference. Both readings are bad for this proposal.

**Leading indicator.** In the first design partner, measure `extraction $ spend ÷ (seats × list price)`. If that ratio exceeds ~0.35 in month one — and the model above says it starts near 1.0–2.7 at Sonnet against a $30 seat — the business is a pass-through with a UI. Watch it *rise* as the customer adopts more agents; that direction of travel is the disqualifying signal, not the level.

**Evidence.** E-C1, E-C2, E-C4, E-C5, E-C6, E-C13–E-C20, E-C26.

### FM-C2 — The incumbents did not wait: this shipped, at zero marginal price, inside seats the buyer already holds

**Mechanism.** I framed this as "an incumbent crushes it the week after it works." The evidence says the week already passed. **Anthropic ships workspace-scoped Memory Stores today** — documented as *"a workspace-scoped collection of text documents"* whose *"writes … stay in sync across sessions that share it,"* with immutable versions, redaction, a REST API, and documented patterns for *"one store per end user, per team, or per project"* (E-C28, public beta 2026-04-08). More pointedly, **"Dreams," shipped 2026-05-06, reorganizes a memory store from transcripts** — that is the proposal's §7 pipeline (raw activity → durable structured knowledge), already a product. The bridge into Claude Code is already in the binary: `CLAUDE_MEMORY_STORES`, "mounted team memory stores in remote sessions," v2.1.172 (E-C29). Around it sit auto memory (E-C7), `/insights` running free extraction over 200 sessions (E-C10), agent teams' shared task list with file-locked claiming (E-C9), org-wide managed CLAUDE.md (E-C11), and Enterprise Analytics (E-C12).

And Anthropic is not alone. **Amp shipped the proposal's own declared strongest wedge — agent handoff (§10) — on 2025-10-23**, with workspace threads readable and searchable by teammates *by default* and a `read_thread` tool to feed them to an agent as context (E-C32); eleven months of head start, at zero markup. **GitHub's Copilot Memory** already shares repo-level facts with everyone holding access, alongside an enterprise agent control plane with agent audit logs (E-C33). **Cursor's Bugbot** has auto-extracted **44,000 learned rules across 110,000 repos** from team PR feedback (E-C34). **Linear already ships the agent-session schema** — `AgentSession` with six states, `AgentActivity` typed as thought/elicitation/action/response/error (E-C35). **Atlassian already sells graph-context retrieval to Claude Code and Codex** via the `twg` CLI at $20/dev/month (E-C36).

The COGS asymmetry is permanent: every one of these runs the identical extraction at marginal cost on inference they already own, where the operator pays retail list (E-C1) — a structural 3–5× disadvantage on the same operation, before distribution is even considered.

**Leading indicator.** `CLAUDE_MEMORY_STORES` appearing in the documented env-vars page; the "machine-local" sentence (E-C8) changing; `/insights` gaining an org scope; Copilot Memory widening from repo to organization. Also watch Anthropic's `/import`, which already ingests Cursor, Copilot, Cline, Windsurf and Devin rule formats into CLAUDE.md — they are consolidating everyone else's context format into theirs, not conceding a neutral layer.

**Evidence.** E-C7–E-C12, E-C28, E-C29, E-C32–E-C36.

### FM-C3 — The free substitute is markdown in git, and the operator already wrote it

**Mechanism.** §5 lists the workarounds — CLAUDE.md/AGENTS.md, HANDOFF.md, ADRs, memory MCPs — and calls them "smaller versions of the problem." That framing is the risk, not the opportunity. They are free, offline, git-versioned, diffable, code-reviewable, greppable by any agent without an integration, and carry zero vendor risk, zero data-egress review, zero marginal token cost. They are already in the repo. §21's own success scenario ("three agents investigated this previously… rejected because it breaks the payments service… approved on March 17") is a decision record — a file format from 2011.

**The "500-line MCP server plus markdown-in-git" hypothesis is not a hypothesis; it shipped, repeatedly.** `basic-memory` is persistent agent memory backed by **plain markdown files over MCP** (3,869★, E-C23). `akitaonrails/ai-memory` does long-term memory for coding CLIs *with vendor-to-vendor handoff* — the one seam I thought might be defensible — at 5,859★, MIT, released two days ago (E-C24). Above them: mem0 64,780★, Graphiti 30,634★, cognee 30,518★, supermemory 29,241★, Letta 24,631★, and a KG memory server inside the official MCP reference repo at 90,106★ (E-C22). PulseMCP counts **765 memory MCP servers**; GitHub returns 3,126 repos (E-C25). A buyer's first question is which of 765 free options this beats and by how much; the honest answer for the §17 MVP is "none yet — we host it."

**The strongest single datapoint is Anthropic's own.** Building a 100k-line C compiler with **16 agents across ~2,000 sessions over two weeks**, Anthropic coordinated shared state **through git, with `current_tasks/` lock files and progress files** (E-C31). The vendor that owns the model, the agent harness, the memory API and the analytics plane reached for markdown in a repo. That is the reference implementation of the free substitute, executed at the exact fleet scale this proposal targets, by the party best positioned to need something better.

**And the operator has already shipped the free substitute themselves.** `agentwaves` implements the session-handoff protocol, `wave-state.md` as authoritative state, velocity and capacity logs, and a 17-invariant session-close script — MIT, public, claiming *"~65-95k tokens per session-start"* saved (E-C21). If that discipline captures most of the value at zero marginal cost, the paid product lives on the residual: cross-machine sync, cross-repo query, access control. That is a hosting business, and it is precisely what FM-C2's incumbent gives away.

**The one thin spot is the most damning.** Decision records — the proposal's most distinctive primitive (§8, §21) — are genuinely underserved: `adr-tools` has 5,669 stars and **no commit since 2020-03-30**; log4brains stalled in 2024; the only agent-native ADR project has 12 stars (E-C27). Read that as a market verdict, not an opening. ADRs have been free, trivial, and widely endorsed for fifteen years and orgs still don't maintain them. The bottleneck was never storage — it was that nobody wants to write or read them. Automating the writing does not fix the reading, and §8 requires a *human* to approve each decision before it becomes authoritative. This is the closest available natural experiment for the core primitive, and it ran fifteen years with a negative result.

**Leading indicator.** In the §18 cold-vs-informed experiment, add a **third arm**: agent B with a hand-written handoff markdown file committed to the repo, and optionally a fourth running `basic-memory` off the shelf. If arm 3 lands within ~80% of arm 2 (structured record), there is no product — there is a template. Run this arm first; it is the cheapest possible kill test and it costs one afternoon. Second indicator: whether design partners' humans ever *approve* a proposed decision unprompted, or whether the approval queue silently fills the way ADR directories do.

**Evidence.** E-C21, E-C22, E-C23, E-C24, E-C25, E-C27; proposal §5, §8, §21 (self-evidence). Marginal-cost claim: reasoned argument — a git-committed markdown file has zero inference cost by construction.

### FM-C4 — Category confusion: the buyer already has a system of record, and "underlying state layer" is a seller's abstraction, not a buyer's budget line

**Mechanism.** §4 instructs: *"Do not build a better Jira integration. Build the underlying state layer that Jira and Linear can consume."* No engineering org has a line item called "underlying state layer." They have one called Linear ($16/seat, E-C13) and one called Copilot/Cursor/Claude (E-C14–E-C16). A third line must be sold against a budget DX puts at **$200–600/engineer/month already committed** — and currently propped up by promotional credits that expire (E-C19).

**This exact pitch has a body count.** Pivotal Tracker sold "a real-time, single source of truth" for seventeen years and was decommissioned in 2025; Almanac sold "a beautiful, organized Single Source of Truth" and shut down the same month; CodeStream was bought for $28.6M and is being switched off this November; Code Climate Velocity dissolved into consulting and its product URL now 404s (E-C38). Swimm — code knowledge for engineering orgs, the nearest neighbour to this proposal — **stopped being a software company four months ago**: *"From Software as a Service to Service as Software"* (E-C39). And when a state layer does succeed, **it exits to the tracker's owner**: DX went to Atlassian for ~$1B, disclosing that *"95%+ of DX customers use at least one Atlassian app"* (E-C40). LeanIX went to SAP; Stepsize to ClickUp. The layer beneath the system of record is not a market; it is an acquisition path for the system of record.

**And the differentiation has already evaporated.** Every surviving vendor in engineering intelligence, internal developer portals, and data catalogs has, as of today, rewritten its homepage around context-for-AI-agents — Roadie is literally selling *"Engineering Context for AI Agents"*; Port is an *"Agentic SDLC Platform"*; Cortex is *"Mission Control for the AI Software Factory"* (E-C42). These are funded companies with existing enterprise customers, sales teams, and SOC 2 reports, arriving at the operator's positioning from a stronger start. TechCrunch's word for the category is *"flooded."*

Worse still, the positioning inverts the power relationship with both parties whose cooperation it needs. Telling Linear it is a *projection* of your state is a declaration that you intend to demote their product — and Linear already owns the work graph, the ownership field, the approver, and (E-C35) the agent-session schema.

**Leading indicator.** In discovery, ask which budget line pays and who signs. "We'd have to create a new line" means a category-creation sale — 4–8 quarters of evangelism before the first dollar, against 6sense's finding that **95% of B2B deals are won by a vendor already on the buyer's Day-One shortlist** (E-C44). Second: if design partners install it and keep writing HANDOFF.md anyway, the record is not authoritative and never will be.

**Evidence.** E-C13, E-C14, E-C15, E-C16, E-C19; proposal §4 (self-evidence). Historical analogues: see Negative results — verification pending.

### FM-C5 — The category demands assets a solo operator cannot supply, in an order that starves the product

**Mechanism.** This ingests raw agent transcripts — source code, secrets, customer data, internal decisions. The proposal flags it itself (§19: "sensitive info in sessions; what never leaves the machine; permission propagation; cross-team visibility"). The gate list above ~30 people is fixed, and now quantified: **SOC 2 Type II costs $20k–$150k (median ~$30k) from an independent auditor and takes close to a year of elapsed time** for a six-month report; 65% of orgs say buyers demand more compliance proof than before; SSO/SAML is such a reliable enterprise gate that dev-tool vendors price it at 208–425% premiums or hide it behind "Call Us!" (E-C44). Add RBAC propagating repo ACLs, data residency, DPA, audit log, retention controls, and a questionnaire per customer. **None produces a unit of the product's value; all precede the first invoice.** Meanwhile the average B2B cycle is **10.1 months**, and >$100k ACV runs 3–9 months (E-C44).

Simultaneously it needs **eleven live integrations** (§15: Claude Code, Codex, OpenCode, Cursor, GitHub, GitLab, Slack, Linear, Jira, CI/CD, production) against formats controlled by competitors shipping weekly. Claude Code's docs alone carry version-gated behavior changes at v2.1.178, .186, .198, .199, .207, .211, .214, .216, .222, .234, .236, .239, .242, .246, .248, .251, .260 (E-C7, E-C9) — the *maintenance* rate for **one** of eleven inputs, from a vendor with every incentive to close the surface. The proposal's Risk #6, "integration complexity eats the company," is not a risk here; it is the base rate.

**The comparables are unambiguous about what happens to tiny teams in this shape.** SourceHut — a git forge, i.e. a genuine system of record, built by one person growing to three — took **~5 years to reach only ~$11k/mo**, and in its last published year the *platform* earned $132,226 against $367,810 of consulting: the product was 26% of revenue, and 11% of users paid. Patrick McKenzie's B2B workflow SaaS reached **~$6,500 MRR after five years** — *"one long bloody slog"* — while his single-player B2C utility out-earned it (E-C43). The solo successes in the same datasets (Plausible, Bannerbear, Tailwind UI) are all **single-player utilities**. The two that plateaued are the two multi-player, integration-heavy ones, and both had to be subsidized. This proposal is squarely in the second category.

The distribution gap is measured: the operator's public artifact expressing this exact thesis has **3 stars, 0 forks** (E-C21), while the closest funded analogue needed **$30M and 34 people to reach a $29 seat** (E-C26). Even Sourcegraph, with $223M raised, ended up monetizing its intelligence layer only at **"$16K minimum annual contract, contact sales"** — and both founders left it for the agent company (E-C41). The operator's depth is real and well-matched to graph/provenance modeling. But the missing assets are **distribution, enterprise trust, and sustained integration maintenance**, and none is a modeling problem.

**Leading indicator.** Time from "design partner says yes" to first invoice. If the first two route through a security questionnaire before pilot, the operator is running a compliance program, not a product. Second: hours on ingestion-format breakage vs. retrieval quality — if the former exceeds the latter by month three, the treadmill has won.

**Evidence.** E-C7, E-C9 (version-churn rate), E-C21; proposal §15, §19, Risk #6 (self-evidence). SOC 2 cost/duration figures: not verified — see Negative results.

### FM-C6 — Timing: the org this product is built for does not demonstrably exist, and the burn runs at present-day prices

**Mechanism.** §1 is explicitly future-conditional: *"A future engineering organization might contain 10–12 human engineers + 100–200+ specialized coding agents."* Everything downstream is load-bearing on that org existing. **The best available primary data says it does not, by two orders of magnitude.** OpenAI's own Codex telemetry paper reports that **67.4% of organizational users run no concurrency at all**, that only *">10% of users manage three or more concurrent Codex agents,"* and that even *inside OpenAI* just *"28.6% managed five or more concurrent agents"* (E-C30). Anthropic's own flagship multi-agent demonstration — a 100k-line C compiler — used **16 agents, not 100–200** (E-C31), and Anthropic's guidance for its own agent-teams feature reads *"Start with 3-5 teammates"* and *"Three focused teammates often outperform five scattered ones"* (E-C9). Every one of these numbers comes from a vendor with maximal commercial incentive to report the opposite.

The 16-agent C compiler carries a second sting for the whole proposal: those agents **coordinated through git, using `current_tasks/` lock files and progress files** (E-C31). When Anthropic itself needed shared state across a 16-agent fleet running 2,000 sessions, it used markdown and git — not a system of record. See FM-C3.

Meanwhile the burn is denominated in today's prices. Building the §17 MVP means running extraction over real corpora continuously during development: at 34.3M tokens/dev/month (E-C5), dogfooding alone costs $43–$215/month per developer-equivalent of corpus before a customer exists, and every §18 re-run repeats it. If the target org arrives in 2028, that is ~24 months of R&D inference, eleven-surface integration maintenance, and compliance work on unknown runway — during which FM-C2's incumbents close the gap for free.

**Leading indicator.** Ask every design-partner candidate one number: how many concurrent agent sessions does your median engineer run in a week, and how many six months ago? A small single digit, roughly flat, means this is a 2028 pain. Second: whether anyone volunteers "coordination between agents" as a top-three problem *before* being prompted with the framing.

**Evidence.** E-C5, E-C6, E-C9; negative result on the 10:100 org (below).

## What would change my mind

Ordered by how decisively each would break my case.

1. **A measured extraction cost below ~$5/dev/month at acceptable quality.** Concretely: a filter discarding >95% of transcript before the LLM sees it (diff-triggered, decision-marker-triggered, or a small local classifier), demonstrated on the 40M-token corpus in E-C5 with recall measured against hand-labeled decisions. If extraction runs on 1–2M tokens/dev/month instead of 34.3M, FM-C1 collapses and everything else becomes arguable. **This is the highest-value experiment available and it costs one weekend on data already on the operator's disk.**
2. **The §18 experiment with a markdown-handoff control arm, where the structured record wins by a wide margin** on a metric a buyer recognizes (human interventions per task, wrong-approach rate). Within 80% and FM-C3 stands: there is no product, only a template.
3. **A buyer who names the budget line unprompted** — one VP Eng saying "this comes out of X" where X is not "we'd have to create it." That breaks FM-C4. Stronger still: a buyer who has *already* evaluated Roadie, Port or Cortex on their new agent-context positioning (E-C42) and rejected them for a reason this product actually addresses.
4. **Telemetry contradicting E-C30** — a first-party dataset showing median concurrency above ~5, or a named org at 10:100 with a number and a source. That breaks FM-C6 and strengthens the rebuttal to FM-C1 (higher volume also implies higher willingness to pay, if the pain is acute enough).
5. **A defensible input the incumbents cannot reach.** The candidate is cross-vendor state (Claude + Codex + Cursor in one org) — Anthropic will never sync OpenAI's sessions. But it is weaker than it looks: `akitaonrails/ai-memory` ships vendor-to-vendor handoff free at 5,859★ (E-C24); Anthropic's `/import` already ingests Cursor, Copilot, Cline, Windsurf and Devin formats (E-C7); and Atlassian's `twg` CLI already feeds one graph to Claude Code, Codex, Gemini *and* Cursor (E-C36) — the neutral position is taken. This needs a buyer naming cross-vendor fragmentation as a felt, ranked pain, not an inference from §6.
6. **Cost-plus pricing that still works.** If the product at Amp/Linear-style zero-markup economics (E-C18, E-C35) is still worth building — as a wedge to a different business, say — my margin argument targets the wrong thing. But say so explicitly, because it changes what "success" means.

## Negative results

Recorded honestly; absence of evidence is stated as such, not converted into a claim.

- **No vendor publishes an "N agents per developer" headline ratio, and my first search pass produced only SEO content-farm pages** with unattributed multipliers, which I decline to cite. Queries: `engineering team running "100 agents" per developer 2026 case study`; `DX research 2026 how many AI agent sessions per developer median parallel agents`. DX's *The AI-Native Developer* (https://getdx.com/blog/the-ai-native-developer/, 2026-05-14; n>1,300 developers) is methodologically credible but contains **no per-developer agent-count figures**; JetBrains' Aug 2026 survey (n>15k) reports share-of-code-by-agent but no concurrency; Faros AI's 2026 report likewise. The gap was ultimately closed by **primary vendor telemetry** (E-C30) and Anthropic's own 16-agent build (E-C31) — both of which land two orders of magnitude below the thesis. **A steel-man should note that E-C30's Codex sample skews toward newer/cloud users and may understate CLI power-user concurrency**, which is the strongest available attack on FM-C6.
- **Gross-margin benchmark: the 2026 attributions do not survive checking.** A search summary attributed 50–60% AI gross margins to a "Bessemer February 2026 pricing playbook" and an "ICONIQ 2026 survey"; the citing page (https://avanteventures.com/en/library/ai-startup-gross-margin-benchmark-2026, read 2026-09-06) in fact cites **only a16z 2020**. I therefore use a16z 2020 (E-C20) and treat the 2026 figures as unverified. **This cuts against me** — a six-year-old benchmark is weaker support than a current one.
- **Fetch failures and second-hand sources, disclosed.** `openai.com`, `help.openai.com`, `sourcegraph.com/docs` and `sourcegraph.com/pricing` return HTTP 403 from this environment; two OpenAI items (the Codex "almost everything" launch post, the Company Knowledge help article) rest on **Wayback snapshots**, dated inline in E-C30's source cluster. **No vendor docs page at Anthropic, OpenAI, GitHub, Cursor, Linear, Amp or Atlassian carries a last-updated date**, so undated doc claims are "as read 2026-09-06" and dated ones come from changelogs/release notes. SaaStr's ACV bands are a practitioner rule of thumb, not a survey. Likewise **no published statistic quantifying "% of enterprise buyers require SOC 2 before purchase"** exists — Vanta's and Drata's own SOC 2 pages are qualitative. The 65% figure I cite is about compliance demonstration generally, not SOC 2 as a hard gate. Queries tried: `percent of enterprise buyers require SOC 2 before purchase survey statistic`; `enterprise buyers require SSO SAML before purchase percent survey`.
- **No base-rate dataset exists for solo-founder enterprise-infrastructure outcomes.** E-C43 is n≈2 documented plateaus against a self-reported, survivorship-biased sample of solo successes. It is suggestive, not statistical, and a steel-man is entitled to say so. No founder postmortem titled "why our developer knowledge tool failed" was found under that framing; the closest primary artifacts are Almanac's shutdown letter and Swimm's exit-from-software post (E-C38, E-C39).

Gartner's "survivability of standalone SEI vendors" line appears only in secondary summaries and is not cited. **Correction to my own framing:** Sourcegraph did *not* "pivot from code search to Amp" — the company split in two on 2025-12-02 and the founders left for Amp (E-C41). I had the shape wrong before checking.
- **Two huge OSS star counts in this space appear inorganic and are excluded.** `GitNexus` (47,076★) and `DeusData/codebase-memory-mcp` (42,425★, repo created 2026-02-24) show star-to-watcher ratios far outside norms (42k stars / 175 watchers in six months) plus byte-identical README clones under other accounts. E-C22–E-C24 use only repos with credible multi-year profiles. Also note two 2026 renames (`ruvnet/claude-flow` → `ruflo`; `campfirein/cipher` → `byterover-cli`) that make older competitive analyses point at moved targets. Cline's "Memory Bank" is a docs pattern inside `cline/cline` (67,567★), not verified today. Amp Enterprise $59/seat and Pieces Pro $10/mo come from third-party sources (weavai, G2, aisotools), not vendor pages, and are excluded from the cost model; only vendor-page figure E-C18 is used.

FM-C5 and FM-C6 argue about the *shape* of the required work — compliance program, eleven-surface treadmill, timing gap — not about the operator lacking time. If the answer is "40 hrs/week, 24 months runway," FM-C6 weakens considerably and FM-C5 partially. **FM-C1 and FM-C2 are unaffected by hours or money**, which is why they are ordered first.
