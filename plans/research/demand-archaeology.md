## Spending found, by category

### 1. Paid products
Real, and concentrated in one shape: **retrieval over existing org artifacts, sold per seat to mid-size engineering orgs.** Unblocked is the only vendor in this space with a public customer wall, and it is a substantial one — 14 named logos, several with 100–350 engineers, several with quantified outcomes they let a vendor publish (E-D1). At $29/user/mo, Fetch's 350 engineers is ~$122k/yr of committed spend. Nobody found publishes ARR, and G2/Capterra review counts were unretrievable (403).

The **"team/shared memory" SKU exists in three places and behaves very differently in each**: cmem.ai charges a **17× multiple over its solo tier** ($333 vs $20/seat/mo) for shared memory (E-D4) — a price with no visible buyer; Warp gates cross-harness agent memory to Enterprise-only, above a $50/user/mo Business tier (E-D6); Letta charges **nothing extra** for sharing (E-D7). Three vendors, three incompatible reads on whether shared memory is a premium good. That dispersion is itself a finding: the market has not priced this because it has not sold it.

MintMCP is the only vendor found with a **stated paying-customer count** in this adjacency — 40+ in six months, with named logos and a 6→12 FTE hiring response (E-D8). What they sell is not memory. It is identity, access control and audit logs for agents.

### 2. Labor
**This is a clean negative, and it is the strongest negative in the document.** I pulled the complete comment trees for six consecutive HN "Who is hiring?" threads (April–September 2026), 1,715 top-level job posts, and counted: **one** mention of CLAUDE.md or AGENTS.md, and it is an intern req that merely names the tool (E-D11). Zero roles for context engineering, agent knowledge curation, or coding-agent governance. Two agent-ops titles total in six months (E-D13). Companies are not hiring humans to maintain agent context.

The exception is one level up: **Scribd is hiring a Lead PM to own an internal agent platform for 200+ engineers, whose scope explicitly includes "the evals, canonical context, and conventions that make agents reliable"** (E-D14). That is one req, at one company, and it is a platform-PM role, not a context-maintainer role.

### 3. Services
A market exists with published rates — **$100–300/hr, with CLAUDE.md authoring named as a line-item deliverable** and packaged engagements from 3-day audits to 6-month embeds (E-D20). Live Upwork and Fiverr listings for Claude Code team setup and training are visible in search indexes but their prices and order counts are behind bot blocks (E-D21), so I cannot state volume. Above that, **Anthropic has put $100M into the partner/services channel** and Accenture alone is training 30,000 people (E-D22); Cognition sells Devin through Infosys, Synechron and LTM (E-D23). Services money around coding agents is large and growing — but it is being spent on *rollout and enablement*, not on a knowledge substrate.

### 4. Internal builds — **the strongest revealed preference found**
- **Shopify / Aquifer + River**: 7,000+ internal users, 59,918 sessions in 30 days, 3,536 merged co-authored PRs, sessions as durable append-only Postgres event logs, and an explicit compounding loop — *"One person's hard-won fix becomes the next person's starting point"* (E-D16).
- **Ramp / Inspect**: **5.5 FTE dedicated team** (4 engineers + director + part-time PM) with 150+ engineers contributing (E-D15). This is the only public, precise headcount figure; fully loaded that is roughly $1.5–2.5M/yr of committed labor.
- **Stripe / Minions**, **Coinbase / Cloudbot** — independently converged architectures (E-D17).
- **SAP SE**: the single closest match to this proposal ever published — shared organizational memory for coding agents, with contributor-approval gates and per-memory provenance (contributor, team, source category), 1,144 curated memories from 900 contributed learnings (E-D18). And its own authors concede *"effects on retrieval and coding tasks remain under evaluation."*
- **Anthropic**'s own 16-agent C-compiler build coordinated through git lock/progress files (E-C31, prior wave).

Note what these builds are: **agent runtimes, sandboxes and session substrates.** Only SAP built a *memory* system. Nobody built a decision ledger with approvals and rejected alternatives — the unserved intersection this proposal targets — except SAP, which built the approval gate but for *knowledge contribution*, not for *decisions*.

### 5. Governance / compliance
Money is being spent, but on the **preventive** side. MintMCP's 40+ paying customers buy blast-radius control, identity, and activity audit logs for Claude Code / Codex agents (E-D8). Vanta — the obvious owner of the retrospective side — has an AI Governance product covering coding agents and ISO 42001 / EU AI Act, and it is **waitlist-only, not GA, no pricing** (E-D19). Stack Internal ships an authorship/timestamp/reliability metadata layer into Copilot and Cursor for Mercado Libre, Xerox and Eli Lilly (E-D9).

I found **no vendor selling, and no buyer paying for, "which human approved what an agent decided, and what alternatives were rejected."** That is the proposal's §8/§9 core and it has no market price.

### 6. The operator's own segment (1–5 engineers)
Weakest category. Evidence that tiny teams pay for *anything* team-shaped is thin and small-ticket: Linear's free tier holds 250 issues and unlimited members, and the first paid step is $10/user/mo (E-D24). Every product above that targets 100+ engineer orgs — Unblocked's published wall is 100–350 engineer companies (E-D1); Warp's team memory sits above a 25-seat Business tier (E-D6); cmem's Team Cloud floor is 3 seats at $333 each, i.e. **$12k/yr for a three-person team**, which is roughly Claude Code seat spend again. No survey, review count, or customer list found shows a 1–5 engineer team buying shared engineering-knowledge software.

## Candidate buyer segments, ranked by strength of revealed spend

**1. Platform / Developer-Experience leads at 150–500-engineer product companies that have already deployed a coding agent org-wide.**
*Current spend:* 4–6 dedicated FTEs on internal agent platforms (Ramp, E-D15), a dedicated PM req (Scribd, E-D14), and/or $19–29/user/mo to Unblocked (E-D1). *Where they congregate:* HN Who-is-Hiring platform reqs, engineering blogs (shopify.engineering, Pragmatic Engineer), Stripe/Ramp/Coinbase alumni networks, LeadDev/DX-adjacent events. *Contactable:* yes — Scribd's hiring manager posts under his own handle; Ramp's team is named in a public article. *Caveat that must be stated:* this segment **builds**, and its build is a runtime, not a record. Selling them a record means beating an in-house team that already owns the session data.

**2. CTO / technical co-founder of a 15–80 person, Series A/B product company, where the CTO is personally the context bottleneck.**
*Current spend:* $19–29/user/mo to Unblocked. Rally is the archetype and the buyer is named: **Alec Robins, CTO & co-founder**, whose stated purchase logic is *"it wasn't worth our time to cobble that together internally"* and whose measured return is *"five to ten hours a week"* of his own time plus a 90% drop in product questions he personally fields (E-D2). This is the only segment where I found a named human, a title, a price, and a first-person reason to buy.
*Where they congregate:* **the 58 employers in E-D12** — companies that publicly stated within the last six months that they run Claude Code daily, each with an apply link or a founder's email in the post (pganalyze's founder&CEO publishes his address directly). Plus Unblocked's own customer wall, r/ClaudeCode and r/ClaudeAI, and the Claude Code Discord.
*This is the answer to the output requirement:* the operator can pull 20 named companies out of E-D12 this week — Pagelove, Tecuity, Biobase, Sunspell, Checkly, pganalyze, JustPark, Alaffia Health, Dashdoc, ArtCraft, Talonic, Authorium, Veedma, VersaFeed, Cora AI, Portless, Deep Core Technology, vvd, Mattermost, AiMi — and address the CTO/founder directly, with the opener "you said publicly that Claude Code does substantial implementation work; what happens when a session ends?"

**3. Enterprise IT / security leadership buying agent governance.**
*Current spend:* MintMCP's 40+ paying customers in six months, incl. Braze, Coursera, Stability AI (E-D8). *Where they congregate:* MCP/agent-security circles, SF Bay enterprise-AI meetups. *Caveat:* the JTBD they pay for is prevention (what may an agent touch), not accountability (who approved what it concluded). Selling them a decision ledger is a different sale to the same budget, and Vanta — better positioned — has not shipped it yet (E-D19).

**4. Tiny startups, 1–5 engineers (the operator's own segment). Not a buyer segment on the evidence available.** Every product priced for shared memory prices above them; zero job postings; no survey or customer list places them as buyers of engineering-knowledge software. They are a design partner pool, not a revenue segment.

## What the spending says about which JTBD is actually bought

**Bought:** *"understand the organizational history of unfamiliar code, and stop routing every question through one person"* — proposal JTBD #6, with #3 as a side-effect. Every quantified customer outcome on Unblocked's wall is onboarding time (RB Global: 3 months instead of 6), question deflection (Rally: 90%), or per-engineer hours (Drata: 1–2/day). Stack Internal sells the same job to enterprises (E-D9). SAP built the same job internally (E-D18).

**Bought, but as prevention:** *accountability* — JTBD #7. MintMCP has 40+ paying customers for agent identity, access limits and activity audit logs (E-D8). The money is real; the shape is a gate, not a ledger. Nobody pays for "who approved this agent's recommendation, and what was rejected."

**Built, never bought:** *coordination* — JTBD #5. Shopify, Ramp, Stripe, Coinbase and Anthropic all spent engineering headcount on it and all built session substrates and sandboxes rather than buying anything (E-D15/16/17). The buy-signal here is negative: the companies with the most acute version of this problem chose to own it.

**Neither bought nor built: handoff** — JTBD #1/#2, the proposal's own §10 "strongest potential initial use case." Handoff demand has fully drained into free software: claude-mem at 93,337 stars and 18,841 weekly installs (E-D5), with its commercial team tier priced at $333/seat and no visible customer (E-D4). Warp has the cross-harness version and has not made it generally available (E-D6). Letta prices sharing at zero premium (E-D7). This confirms and hardens the prior wave's finding rather than softening it: **the operator's two sharpest personal pains — re-briefing a new session, and decisions being forgotten — are the two jobs with the least evidence of anyone paying for a solution.**
